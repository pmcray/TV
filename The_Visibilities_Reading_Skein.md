# A Reading Skein for *The Visibilities*

### Fifty prioritised titles from the US Kindle library, annotated for research

> *A skein: a coiled length of yarn — and a flight of birds in a V. Both apt for a recursive almanac of the seen and the unseen.*

---

## The architecture this skein answers to

*The Visibilities* is, structurally, a **leap-year almanac**: twelve Partitions of roughly thirty-one sections each, totalling **366** — one per day of a turning year. The Partition titles trace that year from *The First Visibility* through the solstice (*The Shortest Shadow*), the harvest (*The Strange Harvest*), the equinox (*The Mental Equinox*), and home to *The Closing Circle*. Procedurally-titled "visibility" sections (the *Optical Void in the Night* / *Recursive Prism of the Mind* grammar) interleave with named content-chapters that cluster into recognisable seams — neutron-scattering physics and the Oxford/Salford/Imperial biography; *2001: A Space Odyssey*; espionage and cipher; PKD and alternate history; Lancashire psychogeography; the table.

The recurring interstitial paragraph is the book's genome — *the map is not the territory*, consistency shattered by a single self-referential remark, every closed loop a begun story. That is the Hofstadterian strange loop your own bibliography marks with two asterisks. The skein below is organised into **twelve strands**, one keyed to each Partition's preoccupation, so the research reading runs parallel to the novel's own turning.

A single title here is already marked *read* on the Kindle (**The Alignment Problem**); it appears as a deliberate re-read, now through the novel's lens.

## Strand I — The Strange Loop
### *for the recursive spine: Gödel, self-reference, mind, "The Mental Equinox," "The Closing Circle"*

1. **Gödel's Proof** — Nagel, Newman & Hofstadter. The load-bearing text. Hofstadter's edition makes incompleteness legible, and your interstitial "consistency is a fragile thing, easily shattered by a single self-referential remark" is essentially its plot. Read first; everything recursive in the book leans on it.
2. **The Human Use of Human Beings** — Norbert Wiener. Cybernetics, feedback, and the human–machine boundary — the deep ancestry of the "Optical Engine" / "Invisible Engine" sections. Wiener's prose on self-regulating systems is a tonal model for the unseen machinery driving the book.
3. **Consciousness: Confessions of a Romantic Reductionist** — Christof Koch. For the sections that turn the lens inward (*Quantum Symmetry of the Mind*, *Strange Symmetry of the Mind*). Useful as the counter-voice to Hofstadter — reduction versus strange-loop emergence.
4. **Inside Jokes** — Hurley, Dennett & Adams. Reverse-engineering the mind via humour; relevant both to the comic interludes (*Sunny Burger*, *SPAM® Day*) and to the self-referential mechanics of why a recursive joke lands.
5. **Wetware: A Computer in Every Living Cell** — Dennis Bray. The cell as computer — the biological substrate beneath the "Engine" and "Resonance" motifs, and a bridge to your own AGI preoccupations.
6. **Networks of the Brain** — Olaf Sporns. The formal connectomics behind "between Levels" and "Hierarchy" titling. Dry but load-bearing if any section literalises a network.
7. **Dark Matter of the Mind** — Daniel L. Everett. The culturally articulated unconscious — apt for a book where Preston, Oxford and Salford are *inherited cognition* as much as geography.

## Strand II — The Seen and the Unseen
### *for the title itself: optics, invisibility, "The First Visibility," "beyond Sight"*

8. **Invisible: The Dangerous Allure of the Unseen** — Philip Ball. The cornerstone of the title's argument: a cultural and scientific history of invisibility, from physics to power. If one book sits closest to the spine of *The Visibilities*, this is it. (You own seven Philip Ball titles; this is the one to prioritise.)
9. **The Hidden Beauty of the Microscopic World** — James Weiss. The unseen made visible at the small scale — direct fuel for *beyond Sight* and *Optical Void* sections.
10. **Hidden Wonders: The Subtle Dialogue Between Physics and Elegance** — Benoît Roman. Physics-as-aesthetics; pairs with the *Prism* / *Symmetry* / *Tesseract* vocabulary and the Garamond-Math typesetting.
11. **How to Read Water** — Tristan Gooley. The art of reading invisible signals off a visible surface — a working method for the "logic of the seen and the unseen unfolds in the space between the lines."

## Strand III — Neutron, Nucleus, Prometheus
### *for the physics/biography seam: Oxford, Salford, Imperial, "Neutron Mirror," "Nuclear Lens"*

12. **American Prometheus** — Bird & Sherwin. Physics, fire, and conscience in one figure — resonant with both the neutron-scattering autobiography and the Promethean register of your work. The Oppenheimer arc is a model for the scientist-as-tragic-protagonist the named chapters keep circling.
13. **The Man from the Future: The Visionary Ideas of John von Neumann** — Ananyo Bhattacharya. The bridge title: von Neumann sits at the join of your novel's physics and your AGI research. Essential for the "Leap of Logic" Partition's intellectual texture.
14. **Geons, Black Holes, and Quantum Foam** — John Archibald Wheeler. A physicist's memoir braided with deep ideas — the autobiographical-plus-physics weave you're attempting, done by a master.
15. **Quantum Legacies: Dispatches from an Uncertain World** — David Kaiser. Short, essayistic physics history; good for seeding the *Quantum* and *Resonance* sections with authentic detail.
16. **Quantum Field Theory, as Simply as Possible** — Anthony Zee. Reference, not cover-to-cover. Keep for verifying any technical conceit so the neutron-scattering material rings true.
17. **Thermodynamic Weirdness: From Fahrenheit to Clausius** — Don S. Lemons. Heat, entropy, irreversibility — quietly underwrites *The Heat Mirage* and *The Data Wash*.

## Strand IV — The Map Is Not the Territory
### *for the recurring epigraph-motif: cartography, "in London," "of Salford"*

18. **How to Lie with Maps** — Mark Monmonier. The definitive demonstration that maps are arguments, not mirrors — the operational manual for your interstitial's central claim.
19. **Einstein's Clocks and Poincaré's Maps** — Peter Galison. A double-strand title: time *and* maps as empires of convention. Directly serves both this strand and Strand VII.
20. **The Red Atlas: How the Soviet Union Secretly Mapped the World** — Davies & Kent. Secret cartography — maps as espionage. Feeds the *Dossier* / *Redacted* sections as much as the cartographic ones.
21. **Maps and Legends: Reading and Writing Along the Borderlands** — Michael Chabon. Essays on genre, reading and writing under the sign of the map — a craft companion that shares your metaphor.
22. **Maps & Civilization: Cartography in Culture and Society** — Norman J. W. Thrower. The historical backbone; consult for period detail when a section grounds itself in a real cartographic moment.

## Strand V — The Infinity of Lists
### *for the almanac form itself: 366 sections, encyclopedias, bounded systems*

23. **On This Date: Discovering America One Day at a Time** — Carl M. Cannon. A day-by-day almanac — the closest formal cousin to your 366-section calendar. Study it as structure, not content.
24. **All the Knowledge in the World: The Extraordinary History of the Encyclopedia** — Simon Garfield. The dream and folly of the bounded total system — exactly the ambition (and joke) of a 366-part compendium of everything.
25. **The Thackery T. Lambshead Cabinet of Curiosities** — VanderMeer (ed.). A faux-encyclopaedic anthology of invented entries — and note the *Lambshead* surname your own bibliography deploys as a fictional interloper. A formal and tonal sibling.
26. **Library: An Unquiet History** — Matthew Battles. The library as a bounded, self-devouring system — apt for a book that contains its own appendices, lore and bibliography.
27. **The Book of Barely Imagined Beings** — Caspar Henderson. A 21st-century bestiary: alphabetical, recursive, digressive. A model for how an entry-based structure can carry an argument.

## Strand VI — Moonwatcher's Defect
### *for the 2001 seam: monoliths, the Moon, "The Moon Walk to the Tree," "Sunshine Desserts in Space"*

28. **2001 between Kubrick and Clarke** — Ulivieri & Odino. Directly on point: the genesis and authorship of the masterpiece your section titles keep saluting (*Moonwatcher's Defect*, *The Local Monolith*, *It Doesn't Look Like Anywhere*). The single best companion to this seam.
29. **The Light of Other Days** — Clarke & Baxter. The "wormcam" makes all of history visible — a Clarke-lineage literalisation of *The Visibilities*' own premise. Read for plot-logic, not prose.
30. **Digital Apollo: Human and Machine in Spaceflight** — David A. Mindell. The actual human-machine negotiation of the Moon landings — grounding for the 2001 register in real engineering and real fallibility.

## Strand VII — The Heat Mirage
### *for time, clocks and perception: the turning year, "Time Warped," the Mirror motif*

31. **The Clock Mirage: Our Myth of Measured Time** — Joseph Mazur. *Mirage* and the measurement of time, named almost exactly as your seventh Partition — and the perfect interrogation of the year-long clockwork the almanac form imposes.
32. **Time Warped: Unlocking the Mysteries of Time Perception** — Claudia Hammond. How subjective time stretches and folds — useful for any section where the daily cadence deliberately distorts.
33. **The Restless Clock** — Jessica Riskin. The centuries-long argument over what makes living things *tick* — mechanism versus life, which is the strange-loop question wearing a clockmaker's coat.

## Strand VIII — The Dossier
### *for espionage, code and redaction: "Cipher," "Protocol," "Ledger," "Traitor," "Ransom Note"*

34. **The Theory That Would Not Die** — Sharon Bertsch McGrayne. Bayes, Enigma, codebreaking — the secret history behind every *Cipher* and *Redacted* section, and a probabilistic counterpart to the book's combinatorial structure.
35. **Talk of the Devil: The Collected Writings of Ian Fleming** — your bibliography already names *From Russia, with Love*; this is the Bond creator's own non-fiction dossier, for texture on the espionage register.
36. **Bull's Eye: The Assassination and Life of Supergun Inventor Gerald Bull** — James Adams. Secret engineering, betrayal and the lone obsessive technician — a real-world template for the conspiratorial machinery the *Engine* and *Dossier* titles imply.

## Strand IX — Now With Added PKD
### *for alternate history and reality-slip: "The Invisible Winds," simulation, the multiverse*

37. **Blueprints of the Afterlife** — Ryan Boudinot. Reality-slippage and recursive simulation in a Pacific-Northwest key — close to home, and to the PKD seam your section titles announce outright.
38. **A New History of the Future in 100 Objects** — Adrian Hon. A future written as artefact-catalogue — a structural cousin to your entry-based almanac, and a tonal study in the documentary-fictional voice.
39. **Memoirs of a Space Traveler: Further Reminiscences of Ijon Tichy** — Stanisław Lem. Recursive, self-undermining SF of the highest order; Lem's Tichy is the patron saint of the unreliable, looping narrator.
40. **The Quantum Curators and the Fabergé Egg** — Eva St. John. Lighter alternate-history time-travel; useful as a contrast case for how *not* to handle the multiverse machinery you're treating more seriously.

## Strand X — A Plain, Unvarnished Preston Man
### *for Deep England and the Lancashire roots: "The Hidden Roots," "Ulysses on the Ribble," psychogeography*

41. **Storyland: A New Mythology of Britain** — Amy Jeffs. British myth re-rooted in landscape — the substrate for the Lancashire and folk material running under the named chapters.
42. **From the Forest: A Search for the Hidden Roots of Our Fairytales** — Sara Maitland. Note the title's exact echo of your fifth Partition, *The Hidden Roots*. The definitive companion to that seam.
43. **We Don't Know Ourselves: A Personal History of Modern Ireland** — Fintan O'Toole. The most rigorous recent model for braiding a single life into a national history — the formal problem the autobiographical chapters are solving.
44. **Storm's Edge: Life, Death and Magic in the Islands of Orkney** — Peter Marshall. Deep-provincial psychogeography where the unseen is taken seriously; a methodological model for "every visibility is a kind of concealment."

## Strand XI — Recursion; see under Recursion
### *for the metafiction of its own making: writing craft, "The Recollections," the poetics of structure*

45. **The Recollections: Fragments From a Life in Writing** — Christopher Priest. Priest is already in your bibliography (*The Affirmation*); his memoir on a recursive, unreliable writing life is the closest craft-model you own to what *The Visibilities* attempts.
46. **Perplexing Plots: Popular Storytelling and the Poetics of Murder** — David Bordwell. The structural grammar of how complex, puzzle-form narratives manage their reader — directly applicable to a 366-part architecture.
47. **Why Read the Classics?** — Italo Calvino. Calvino's recursive thinking about the canon, from the author of *If on a winter's night a traveller* — permission and method for a book that reads itself.

## Strand XII — The Ultraintelligence Engine
### *for the Goodian leap: "The Leap of Logic," AGI, alignment, the intelligence explosion*

48. **The Alignment Problem: Machine Learning and Human Values** — Brian Christian. *(Already read — re-read here.)* Revisit through the novel's lens: the alignment problem *is* the strange loop, a system modelling and revising itself. The hinge between your fiction and your Prometheus work.
49. **The Precipice** — Toby Ord. Existential risk as the discord beneath the "Fantasy of Love and **Discord**." The sober register against which the Promethean ambition of the *Engine* sections plays.
50. **Artificial General Intelligence** (MIT Essential Knowledge) — Julian Togelius. A compact, current grounding in the AGI concepts your *Leap of Logic* Partition gestures toward — keep for accuracy.

## How to walk the skein

Three quick suggestions for sequencing, since fifty titles is itself a small almanac:

- **Read the four cornerstones first** — *Gödel's Proof* (I), *Invisible* (II), *2001 between Kubrick and Clarke* (VI), and *The Alignment Problem* (XII). Together they triangulate the whole: recursion, the unseen, the 2001 lineage, and the Goodian engine.
- **Then read by Partition**, pulling each strand as you draft into the corresponding month of the year. Strand V (the almanac form) is best read *before* you finalise the section-grammar; Strand XI (craft) is best read *during* revision.
- **Hold three in reserve as reference, not reading** — Zee (16), Thrower (22), Togelius (50). Consult to verify; don't let them block the narrative ones.

*Total: 50 titles across 12 strands. One re-read (no. 48); the remaining 49 currently unread on the Kindle.*
